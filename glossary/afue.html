AFUE (Furnace Efficiency)

East Texas HVAC & trades glossary · plain English for homeowners and contractors.

Glossary All 10 terms Definitions and general guidance only — confirm code and rebate rules locally.

Definition

AFUE stands for Annual Fuel Utilization Efficiency. It is the percentage of fuel energy a furnace converts to useful heat over a typical year. A 96 percent AFUE furnace turns about 96 cents of every fuel dollar into heat; the rest goes out the flue.

In plain English

Standard non-condensing gas furnaces start around 80 percent AFUE. Condensing high-efficiency models run roughly 90 to 98 percent.

High-efficiency furnaces extract extra heat from exhaust, which condenses and drains as water, so they vent through PVC and need a condensate drain.

Texas winters are short. The extra cost of a 96-98 percent model takes longer to repay here than in the upper Midwest, so a 80-92 percent model may be the better value for some homes. Be honest about this.

AFUE says nothing about duct leaks or sizing, which also affect real-world comfort and fuel use.

Common mistakes

  • Selling the highest AFUE to everyone regardless of payback.
  • Mixing AFUE with SEER2; they measure different things.
  • Not mentioning that a heat pump may replace the furnace entirely.

If you're a contractor: how to use this on your site

  • Offer a plain comparison: 80 percent vs 92 percent vs 96 percent with a 'when it makes sense' line.
  • Mention the venting and condensate requirements.
  • Link to the heat pump entry.

FAQ

What AFUE should I buy?
It depends on gas price, how many months you heat and your budget. Ask for a payback estimate based on your own bills.

Does AFUE apply to heat pumps?
No. Heat pumps use HSPF2 and COP.
