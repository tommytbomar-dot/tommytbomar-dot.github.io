Ductwork / Duct Sealing

East Texas HVAC & trades glossary · plain English for homeowners and contractors.

Glossary All 10 terms Definitions and general guidance only — confirm code and rebate rules locally.

Definition

Ductwork is the network of supply and return tubes that moves heated or cooled air through a house. Duct sealing is the process of closing leaks and gaps, usually with mastic sealant or approved tape, and insulating ducts in unconditioned space.

In plain English

ENERGY STAR notes that typical duct systems can lose 20 to 30 percent of the air moving through them to leaks and poor connections. In a hot Texas attic that is cooled air dumped outside.

Symptoms: rooms that never get comfortable, high bills, dusty air, a unit that runs constantly. A duct leakage test (with a blower and gauges) measures the problem instead of guessing.

Ducts in attics need insulation. Flex duct that is crushed, sagging or kinked restricts airflow.

Changing equipment without checking ducts often disappoints. Larger or more efficient equipment cannot deliver its rating through restrictive ducts.

Common mistakes

  • Selling 'duct cleaning' as a cure-all for comfort complaints.
  • Promising specific savings percentages without a test.
  • Skipping a leakage test before and after.

FAQ

What is mastic?
A paste sealant brushed on duct seams. It lasts longer than cloth tape.

Is duct sealing a DIY job?
Accessible ducts can be sealed carefully by a handy homeowner, but testing needs equipment.
