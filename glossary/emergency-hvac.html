Emergency HVAC / No-Cool Call

East Texas HVAC & trades glossary · plain English for homeowners and contractors.

Glossary All 10 terms Definitions and general guidance only — confirm code and rebate rules locally.

Definition

An emergency HVAC call is a request for same-day or after-hours service when a system is not cooling or heating and conditions are unsafe or unbearable — typical in an East Texas heat wave or a hard freeze.

In plain English

Emergency demand spikes on the hottest days. Customers search from their phone, and the first business they can reach by phone usually wins the job.

Define what 'emergency' means for your business: 24/7 dispatch, extended hours or next-morning priority. State the surcharge, if any, before the tech rolls.

Vulnerable households — elderly, infants, medical needs — often cannot wait. If you triage, say so on the page.

Missed calls are the biggest leak. A voicemail with a clear promise (we call back within 30 minutes) beats a full mailbox.

Common mistakes

  • Advertising '24/7' while sending calls to voicemail overnight.
  • Hiding the after-hours fee.
  • Not updating Google Business Profile hours for holidays and heat waves.

If you're a contractor: how to use this on your site

  • Put a tap-to-call button in the top of every page and a pinned banner during heat waves.
  • Write the page for the search 'AC not cooling [city]' with a quick checklist (breaker, filter, thermostat, iced coil) and then your number.
  • Link to the drain-line entry — a clogged drain is a frequent no-cool cause.

FAQ

What counts as an HVAC emergency?
No cooling in extreme heat, no heating in a freeze, burning smell, gas smell (call the gas utility first), water leaking into the ceiling.

Why is after-hours service pricier?
Overtime labor and on-call staffing. Ask the price up front.
