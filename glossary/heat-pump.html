Heat Pump

East Texas HVAC & trades glossary · plain English for homeowners and contractors.

Glossary All 10 terms Definitions and general guidance only — confirm code and rebate rules locally.

Definition

A heat pump is a refrigerant-based system that moves heat rather than creating it. In summer it works like an air conditioner; in winter it reverses and pulls heat from outdoor air into the house.

In plain English

Because it moves heat instead of burning fuel, a heat pump can deliver two to three times more heat energy than the electricity it uses in mild weather.

East Texas winters are mild enough that heat pumps handle most days efficiently. Many systems include electric 'auxiliary' or 'strip' heat for the coldest nights and defrost cycles; heavy use of strip heat is expensive, so check the thermostat settings.

Dual-fuel setups pair a heat pump with a gas furnace and switch to gas below a set outdoor temperature.

Cold-climate and inverter models keep capacity at lower temperatures, which matters less in East Texas than in northern states.

Common mistakes

  • Calling it an 'AC' on one page and a 'heat pump' on another so searches split.
  • Ignoring auxiliary heat strips in energy-cost claims.
  • Promising a monthly savings number without the customer's current system and utility rates.

FAQ

What is HSPF2?
The heating efficiency rating for heat pumps under the 2023 test procedure, like SEER2 but for heating.

Does it work when it freezes?
Yes, but efficiency drops as it gets colder and backup heat may run. East Texas freezes are usually short.
