Manual J Load Calculation

East Texas HVAC & trades glossary · plain English for homeowners and contractors.

Glossary All 10 terms Definitions and general guidance only — confirm code and rebate rules locally.

Definition

Manual J is the ACCA (Air Conditioning Contractors of America) standard procedure for calculating how much heating and cooling a specific home needs, based on its construction, insulation, windows, orientation, air leakage and occupants.

In plain English

The calculation produces a heating and cooling load in BTU/hour. That number is what equipment capacity should be matched to.

It is part of a set: Manual J for load, Manual S for selecting equipment, Manual D for duct design. Many building codes and utility rebate programs reference these.

Software does the arithmetic, but the inputs matter: wall type, attic insulation, window U-values and shading, duct location, number of people. Rushing the inputs gives garbage out.

A real Manual J takes an hour or more on site. If a quote shows a size without any measurements taken, the size is probably a guess.

Common mistakes

  • Saying 'we do load calcs' when you actually use a rule of thumb.
  • Hiding the report. A good contractor can show the homeowner the printed results.
  • Skipping Manual D when changing system size, since the old ducts may not carry the new airflow.

If you're a contractor: how to use this on your site

  • Offer a visible sample of a load report (with a fake address labeled SAMPLE) so buyers see what to expect.
  • Put 'Manual J, S and D' under your replacement-process steps if it is true.
  • Link from tonnage and ductwork pages.

FAQ

Who does a Manual J?
Many HVAC contractors and independent energy raters do. Ask whether it is included in the quote.

Is it required?
Many jurisdictions and incentive programs require it for replacements or new construction. Check your local code and the program rules.
