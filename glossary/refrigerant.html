Refrigerant (R-410A / R-454B)

East Texas HVAC & trades glossary · plain English for homeowners and contractors.

Glossary All 10 terms Definitions and general guidance only — confirm code and rebate rules locally.

Definition

Refrigerant is the working fluid circulating in an air conditioner or heat pump, absorbing heat indoors and releasing it outdoors. R-410A has been the standard for residential systems since the 2010s; newer lower-global-warming-potential refrigerants such as R-454B and R-32 are replacing it.

In plain English

Under the AIM Act, EPA is phasing down HFC refrigerants. New residential split systems manufactured on or after January 1, 2025 must use refrigerants with a lower global warming potential. Installation of existing R-410A inventory has a defined sell-through period, so check the current EPA rule text before quoting a deadline.

R-454B and R-32 are classified mildly flammable (A2L). Technicians need appropriate training, tools and procedures. You cannot just top off a system with a different refrigerant.

Handling refrigerant requires EPA Section 608 certification. Do not buy the DIY can: overcharging or undercharging damages compressors.

A system that 'needs refrigerant' usually has a leak. Finding and fixing the leak matters more than topping off.

Common mistakes

  • Telling customers a system 'uses up' refrigerant.
  • Quoting a flat 'R-22 recharge' price; R-22 is long phased out.
  • Skipping mention of the transition when a customer is deciding repair versus replace.

If you're a contractor: how to use this on your site

  • Add a short 'repair or replace' section that mentions the refrigerant type of the existing system.
  • Show your techs' certification honestly.
  • Link to the heat pump and SEER2 entries.

FAQ

Can I switch my R-410A system to R-454B?
No. They are not interchangeable in the same equipment.

Is R-410A going away?
It is being phased out for new equipment. Existing systems keep running and can be serviced.
