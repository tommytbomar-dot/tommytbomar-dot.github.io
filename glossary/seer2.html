SEER2

Definition

SEER2 (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio 2) is the efficiency rating for central air conditioners and heat pumps measured under the updated U.S. Department of Energy test procedure that took effect January 1, 2023. It replaced the older SEER number.

In plain English

The rating is total seasonal cooling output divided by total electricity used. A higher SEER2 means the system uses less electricity to deliver the same cooling.

SEER2 numbers read lower than old SEER numbers for the same equipment because the new test applies more realistic duct resistance. A unit once called '16 SEER' is roughly '15.2 SEER2'. Do not compare a SEER number to a SEER2 number.

Minimums vary by region. Texas falls in the DOE Southeast region, where the minimum for most new split-system residential ACs under 45,000 BTU/h is 14.3 SEER2. Confirm current minimums on the DOE site or with your distributor before printing them on a page.

A higher rating costs more up front. In East Texas, where cooling runs five to seven months a year, the payback math is real but depends on your usage, utility rate and install quality. Poor ducts or wrong sizing can erase the gain.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting old SEER numbers next to new SEER2 numbers on the same page.
  • Promising a specific dollar saving without the home's size, duct condition and electric rate.
  • Listing a SEER2 number you do not install.

If you're a contractor: how to use this on your site

  • On each equipment page, write the SEER2 number and the model line you really sell: 'Our most common install is a 15.2 SEER2 two-stage heat pump.'
  • Add one sentence explaining SEER2 for homeowners comparing quotes, then link to this glossary.
  • Use the term in an H2 so searches like 'what is SEER2' land on you.

FAQ

Is SEER2 higher always better?
Higher means more efficient on paper, but the best choice also depends on sizing, ducts, climate and budget.

Can I still buy SEER units?
Systems are now rated in SEER2. Older SEER numbers still appear on some listings; ask the contractor for the SEER2 figure.
