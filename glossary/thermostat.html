Smart Thermostat / Controls

Definition

A smart thermostat connects to Wi-Fi, learns or follows schedules and can be controlled by phone. Zoning controls split a home into areas with their own temperature settings using motorized dampers.

In plain English

Not all thermostats work with all equipment. Heat pumps, two-stage and variable-speed systems each need compatible controls, and many require a common (C) wire.

Savings are modest and depend on habits. A schedule that raises the setpoint when nobody is home helps; big setbacks on a heat pump can trigger expensive strip heat.

Wi-Fi thermostats can report runtime and fault alerts, which helps a contractor diagnose a failing system.

Zoning is helpful for two-story houses but needs good duct design; it is not a fix for poor ducts.

Common mistakes

  • Promising a percentage saving from a thermostat alone.
  • Installing without checking the C wire and stages.
  • Not showing homeowners how to use it.

FAQ

Do I need a C wire?
Most smart thermostats need continuous power. Some include adapters. A technician can check.

Will a smart thermostat lower my bill?
Possibly a little. Correct setpoints and a healthy system matter more.
