Tonnage (HVAC)

East Texas HVAC & trades glossary · plain English for homeowners and contractors.

Glossary All 10 terms Definitions and general guidance only — confirm code and rebate rules locally.

Definition

In HVAC, a 'ton' is a unit of cooling capacity equal to 12,000 BTU per hour — the heat absorbed by melting one ton of ice over 24 hours. A 3-ton air conditioner removes about 36,000 BTU of heat each hour.

In plain English

Residential systems usually run from 1.5 to 5 tons in half-ton steps. The number says nothing about weight.

Bigger is not better. An oversized system cools the air quickly, shuts off, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out. In East Texas humidity that means a cold, clammy house, mold risk and short-cycling that wears the compressor.

Old rules of thumb like 'one ton per 500 square feet' ignore insulation, windows, sun exposure, duct losses and occupants. The accepted way to pick tonnage is a Manual J load calculation.

Variable-speed and two-stage systems tolerate small sizing errors better, but they still need a proper calculation.

Common mistakes

  • Replacing 'like for like' without checking whether the old unit was oversized.
  • Advertising tonnage by square footage.
  • Using 'ton' and 'weight' interchangeably with customers.

If you're a contractor: how to use this on your site

  • Put 'we size every system with a Manual J load calculation' on your replacement page — only if you do.
  • Explain tonnage in two sentences next to your service-area list.
  • Link to the Manual J glossary page.

FAQ

How many tons do I need for 2,000 sq ft?
There is no honest single answer. It could be 3 tons or 4 depending on the house. Ask for a load calculation.

Does more tonnage cool faster?
It can, but at the cost of poor humidity control and short-cycling.
