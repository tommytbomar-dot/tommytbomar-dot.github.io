Best photo scanners for restoring family photos at home (honest gear guide)

If you want to scan prints before sending them for restoration, scan quality matters more than brand. Here is a practical guide.

Disclosure: I am not enrolled in any affiliate program for the products on this page, and none of the links below carry an affiliate ID. If that changes, I will mark each affiliate link as such on this page. Last reviewed October 2026.

Your three options

  • Phone scan (free): Google PhotoScan or a plain camera shot in even light. Fine for sharing, adequate for light restoration of small prints.
  • Flatbed photo scanner (best quality per dollar): typically Epson Perfection or Canon CanoScan LiDE series. Look for 1200+ dpi optical resolution and a flat glass bed. Epson V600-class models also scan negatives and slides.
  • Sheet-fed photo scanner (speed): Epson FastFoto-class devices scan stacks quickly. Good for boxes of prints, less ideal for fragile or curled originals.

Scan settings that help restoration

  • 600 dpi for 4x6 prints; 1200 dpi for wallet-size or if you plan to enlarge
  • Save as TIFF or highest-quality JPEG; do not use auto-fix or dust removal that smooths faces
  • Clean the glass, handle prints by the edges, and scan the back if there is writing
  • Keep originals - always send a copy
