Plumbing emergency SEO & the missed-call problem

When a pipe bursts the customer calls the first plumber who answers. Your page should make 'call now' obvious and honest about prices and response times.

Remote and async. No guarantees on rankings or call volume. No invented case studies — see the honest case-study shell.

What an emergency-plumbing page needs

  • A phone number as a tap-to-call button, visible without scrolling. Repeat it in the sticky header on mobile.
  • Honest availability. Say whether it is 24/7 dispatch or on-call until a certain hour. If the answer is 'next business morning', say so — it saves a bad review.
  • Pricing clarity. After-hours fee, trip charge and hourly rate ranges set expectations. Avoid 'free estimates' if it is not true after hours.
  • Shutoff instructions. A short 'while you wait' list builds trust: locate the main shutoff, turn off the water heater power or gas, open low faucets, move valuables. Link to your city utility's guide if possible.
  • Service area. List cities and zip codes, not just 'Texas'.
  • Licensing. Texas plumbers are licensed by the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. Show your license number; customers can verify it.

Where emergency plumbers lose calls

  • Calls that go to voicemail with no callback promise. Pair this page with the missed-call SMS script pack — consent-first.
  • GBP hours showing 'Closed' while the ad says 24 hours.
  • Contact forms as the only option. During a flood, people call.
  • Slow pages: an emergency page with a 6 MB hero image loses the customer on cellular.

Five-step free fix list

  1. Make the phone number the first thing visible on mobile and test with a real phone.
  2. Set GBP hours to match your real after-hours policy; use 'Open 24 hours' only if you answer.
  3. Add one paragraph on the after-hours fee.
  4. Write a 6-step 'before we arrive' safety list.
  5. Record a voicemail that promises a callback time and give it a test call at 2 a.m.
