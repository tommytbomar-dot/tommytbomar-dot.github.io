Restore faded family photos — tips

Yellowing, scratches, and soft faces are fixable. Waxy “AI plastic skin” is not what you want hanging on the wall. Here’s a practical prep guide.

Scan or photograph the original well

  • Flatbed scan at 300–600 DPI if you can. Phone photos work if the print is lit evenly (no glare, no shadow from your hand).
  • Shoot straight-on; warped corners make faces harder to keep natural.
  • Send the largest file you have — upscaling a tiny JPG later looks muddy.
  • Don’t “fix” the scan yourself with heavy filters before sending; leave room for careful work.

What “natural” restore usually means

  • Color cast correction (that orange/brown fade)
  • Dust, crease, and scratch cleanup
  • Gentle sharpening — not cartoon eyes
  • Printable ~4K output when the source supports it

What we avoid: inventing people who weren’t in the frame, fake before/after galleries, or claiming miracles from a blurry postage stamp photo.

When a photo may not restore well

  • Severe mold that ate facial detail
  • Extreme crop where half a face is gone
  • Screenshots of screenshots (too little signal left)

You’ll get an honest note if a piece isn’t printable quality — better than a surprising bill.
