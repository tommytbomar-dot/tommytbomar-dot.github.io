Veterinary clinic local SEO & client booking path

Pet owners search when they are worried. Fast answers about hours, emergencies and new-client booking decide who gets the call.

Remote and async. No guarantees on rankings or call volume. No invented case studies — see the honest case-study shell.

What pet owners look for first

  • Open now? Hours must be accurate, with a clear line on what you do after hours: refer to an emergency hospital (name it) or take urgent cases until a set time.
  • New client steps. Are you accepting new patients? What do they bring (records, vaccine history)? Can they book online?
  • Services by species. Dogs and cats are assumed; say plainly if you see exotics, birds, horses or livestock. Rural East Texas practices often cover large animals — a separate page helps.
  • Pricing transparency. A starting price for a wellness exam and common vaccines reduces price-shock calls.
  • Fear-free or low-stress handling. Only claim certification you hold, and say what you actually do (separate entrances, calming protocols).

Emergency and after-hours clarity

  • Give the nearest 24-hour emergency vet's name and phone on your contact page and in your GBP description.
  • Poison control: the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center number is a useful line to include; say that a consultation fee may apply.
  • Voicemail script: open with the emergency instruction, then the callback promise. The after-hours voicemail script pack has industry-specific examples.

Five-step free fix list

  1. Add a 'What to do in a pet emergency' box with your after-hours plan.
  2. Create a new-client page listing what to bring and how to request records transfer.
  3. Check the booking tool on a phone; remove any required account creation.
  4. Confirm GBP categories (Veterinarian; add Emergency veterinarian only if true).
  5. Post a seasonal tip (heartworm prevention, heat safety, fireworks anxiety) to GBP each month.
